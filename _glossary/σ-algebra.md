## Definition
Let $X$ be a [[set]]. A collection $\mathcal{Y}$ of subsets of $X$ is a **σ-algebra** over $X$ if
1. $\emptyset \in \mathcal{Y}$ (*empty set* in $\mathcal{Y}$),
2. $X \setminus Y \in \mathcal{Y}$  for all $Y \in \mathcal{Y}$, and
3. $\cup_{i \in \mathbb{N}} Y_i \in \mathcal{Y}$ for every sequence $(Y_i)_{i \in \mathbb{N}}$ of elements in $\mathcal{Y}$.

By [[De Morgan's laws]], *σ-algebra* also closed under countable intersection. 

(short version: given set $X$, *σ-algebra* is closed under unions and intersections of countable families of subsets)

## Definition 
A **field** $\mathcal{F}$ is a class of *sets* such that
1. if $A \in \mathcal{F}$, then $A^c \in \mathcal{F}$ (the *complement* of the set $A$ is also in the *field*) 
2. if $A \in \mathcal{F}$, $B \in \mathcal{F}$, then $A \cup B \in \mathcal{F}$

other properties follow
- $S \in \mathcal{F}$ (where $S$ is the *universal set*)
- $\emptyset \in \mathcal{F}$ (where $\emptyset$ is the *empty set*)
- $A \cap B \in \mathcal{F}$ 
- etc.

## Notes
- if $S$ is any collection of subsets of $X$, then we can always find a *σ-algebra* containing $S$, namely the *power set* of $S$
- take the *intersection* of all *σ-algebras* containing $S$ to obtain the smallest such *σ-algebras*, the *σ-algebra generated by S*
- known as a *σ-field* or simply *field* in some literature (but not to be confused with the *field* from field theory/algebra or *vector field*... terminology is ambiguous)
