## Definition

Let $X$ be a [[set]]. A collection $\mathcal{Y}$ of subsets of $X$ is a **σ-algebra** over $X$ if

1. $\emptyset \in \mathcal{Y}$ (_empty set_ in $\mathcal{Y}$),
2. $X \setminus Y \in \mathcal{Y}$ for all $Y \in \mathcal{Y}$, and
3. $\cup_{i \in \mathbb{N}} Y_i \in \mathcal{Y}$ for every sequence $(Y_i)_{i \in \mathbb{N}}$ of elements in $\mathcal{Y}$.

By [[De Morgan's laws]], _σ-algebra_ also closed under countable intersection.

(short version: given set $X$, _σ-algebra_ is closed under unions and intersections of countable families of subsets)

## Definition

A **field** $\mathcal{F}$ is a class of _sets_ such that

1. if $A \in \mathcal{F}$, then $A^c \in \mathcal{F}$ (the _complement_ of the set $A$ is also in the _field_)
2. if $A \in \mathcal{F}$, $B \in \mathcal{F}$, then $A \cup B \in \mathcal{F}$

other properties follow

- $S \in \mathcal{F}$ (where $S$ is the _universal set_)
- $\emptyset \in \mathcal{F}$ (where $\emptyset$ is the _empty set_)
- $A \cap B \in \mathcal{F}$
- etc.

## Notes

- if $S$ is any collection of subsets of $X$, then we can always find a _σ-algebra_ containing $S$, namely the _power set_ of $S$
- take the _intersection_ of all _σ-algebras_ containing $S$ to obtain the smallest such _σ-algebras_, the _σ-algebra generated by S_
- known as a _σ-field_ or simply _field_ in some literature (but not to be confused with the _field_ from field theory/algebra or _vector field_... terminology is ambiguous)
